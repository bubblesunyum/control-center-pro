# handoff 2026-08-30

Closed ccp-lr7 — the shell is done, 9/9, built entirely on placeholders.
Unexplained: during the gallery verification the panel dismissed itself in the
middle of a three-click burst and I never worked out which click did it.

The clicks were synthetic (CGEvent, see the `an-agent-can-drive-a-real-drag-with-cgevent`
memory), and two of them landed on the user's Claude window because the panel
had already gone. My assumption at the time was that the gallery popover was
dismissing the panel, and I tested that directly afterwards — it isn't; a click
on the + opens the popover and the panel stays up. So something else in that
burst hid it, and the two candidates I never separated are a click arriving
while the popover's own transient-dismissal shield was up, and the global mouse
monitor from ccp-lr7.4 seeing an event it shouldn't. The second one would be a
real bug in dismissal and I would want to know. It reproduces, if it reproduces,
by clicking fast — the sequence that did it was ~0.6s apart.

Two things I'd look at again with fresh eyes:

The wiggle desynchronises by sleeping a random 0–200ms before starting the
repeatForever, because SwiftUI won't let you give an animation a phase. It works
and it reads fine on screen, but it is a sleep standing in for a phase offset
and I never liked it. If `.delay()` inside `repeatForever` behaves in a later
SDK, that is the honest version.

`--edit-mode` is the second agent-only launch flag on the app after
`--show-panel`. Two is fine. A third means the app has grown a debug surface by
accretion and it wants one mechanism instead of a flag per state.

Next: ccp-8ld is the obvious pickup — the shell is proven and every widget in it
is a skeleton. ccp-8ld.1 (port shared infra from upstream) comes first, and
ccp-8ld.3 (Quick Toggles) now unblocks ccp-lmr, which is the capture the design
reviewer has been asking for since ccp-lr7.5.
